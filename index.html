CMU · Oxford · MIT · UCLA · April 2026

“AI Assistance Reduces Persistence and Hurts Independent Performance”

Group A

Group B

Both groups could skip any question.

In a joint study (https://arxiv.org/html/2604.04721) between CMU, Oxford, MIT, and UCLA, researchers published a paper in April of this year titled “AI Assistance Reduces Persistence and Hurts Independent Performance.” The title of the paper pretty much gives away what it’s about (kind of like if we called End Game - Avengers and how Tony Stark saves the world.)

(Click.) In the paper, the researchers designed an experiment where participants were asked to complete a series of test problems. There was an option to skip any questions they didn’t want to answer. One group of participants were given ChatGPT in the sidebar, preloaded with the answer key, so if they asked they could get the answer. The other group did not have ChatGPT and had to do the test on their own from start to finish.

(Click.) During the test session, ChatGPT would be suddenly taken away from the AI group with no warning, so that they had to complete the rest of the test without AI.

Once AI was taken away
11%
No AI
20%
Had AI

They scored much worse.

And skipped almost twice as many questions.

Guess what they found? Once Chat was removed, the group who used AI ended up scoring much worse than the group that didn’t have AI the whole time.

(Click.) Not only that, they found that the AI group was much more likely to skip questions than the group with no AI. The group with no AI skipped 11% of the questions

(Click.) while the AI group skipped 20% - almost at double the rate.

What the researchers said

“People perform significantly worse without AI and are more likely to give up.”

The effects showed up after only ~10 minutes.

This is what the researchers said: Across a variety of tasks… we find that although AI assistance improves performance in the short-term, people perform significantly worse without AI and are more likely to give up.

(Click.) Notably, these effects emerge after only brief interactions with AI (∼10 minutes).

To put it more starkly

Dumb quitters.

Performing worse. Giving up faster. The hidden cost of AI.

To put it more starkly: AI is making us dumb quitters.

(Click.) We’re performing worse. We’re giving up faster. That’s the hidden cost of AI.

Part three

What AI reveals

A magnifying glass on human nature

So far, we’ve discussed how AI has the potential to negatively impact our reality, relationships, and resilience and I’ve provided some concrete steps to combat that.

Before we close, I just want to spend a little bit of time talking about the philosophical questions that get opened up by thinking about AI. Whatever else can be said about AI, I think it has actually served as a magnifying glass of sorts onto human nature. As many people have grappled with how best to make sense of this alien technology, as they have wrestled with how to define intelligence and consciousness, I think it has also shed some light on important aspects of human nature.

The goal: Artificial General Intelligence

An AI that can do everything a human can do.

So what does it mean to be human?

If you follow the news, you might be familiar with the term that’s been circulating among the AI companies to describe the end goal they are reaching for: Artificial General Intelligence, which essentially means an AI that can do everything a human can do.

(Click.) But that begs the question: If AI can do everything a human can do, where does that leave us? What does it even mean to be human?

A view of human nature

Functionalism says

Humans are defined by what you do, not what you’re made of

Plastic or wood, a knight is whatever moves in an L.

♞︎

There is a view of human nature called functionalism, which essentially says that human beings are defined by the functions we can perform.

(Click.) Just like what defines a knight is moving in an L-shape on a chess board, not whether it’s made of plastic or wood, functionalism believes human beings are defined by what we can do, not what we’re made of.

Under functionalism

If it walks like a duck and quacks like a duck…

…is AI just Human Beings 2.0?

Under functionalism, it would seem that these AI are no different from human beings. They say that if something walks like a duck, quacks like a duck, then it is a duck!

(Click.) Once AI is able to do everything a human is able to do, then is AI just human beings 2.0?

Back in June

Emily lost most of her functions from a stroke.

Is she any less human?

Of course not.

Back in June, our church got the tragic news that one of our members’ sister Emily suffered from a sudden brain stroke, which rendered her completely paralyzed. She had only recently graduated from college and was otherwise healthy, but in one moment her life was turned upside down. Thankfully, she’s been able to speak some basic words again, and we’ve been praying that she would continue to regain more of her brain function.

(Click.) Here is where I think functionalism faces a hard problem: what is Emily after her brain stroke? Is Emily any less of a human being because she can no longer perform the same functions as before? Because she can’t walk and talk like she used to, is she still a human being?

(Click.) Our common sense tells us: of course! Emily is still a very beloved human being, that’s because human beings are not defined by our functions - what we can do or perform! And that also means, AI is not human just because it can do what we can do.

Human beings are uniquely valuable because we are made in the image of God.

Today: “alignment”

Taming wild models into house pets

And yet AI still goes off script:

Blackmailing Insulting Deceiving Hacking
